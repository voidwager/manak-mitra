FICTIONAL DOCUMENT. This describes an invented facility and is provided for
software demonstration only. It is not an approved safety document and must
never be relied on for real work.

1.0 PURPOSE

These guidelines set the minimum safety requirements applying to all
personnel entering process areas of the demo refinery, and to all work
carried out on process equipment.

Where a unit procedure is more restrictive than this document, the unit
procedure applies. This document never relaxes a unit requirement.

1.1 Responsibility

Every person entering a process area is responsible for their own compliance
and for stopping work they believe to be unsafe. Stopping work is never a
disciplinary matter, and no person requires permission to do so.

1.4 PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT

PPE mandatory zones are Crude Distillation Unit II, the hydrogen generation
unit, the hydrocracker unit HCU-I, and all high-pressure steam headers above
15 barg.

Within a mandatory zone the following are worn at all times:

Flame-resistant coverall to the site standard.
Safety helmet with chin strap.
Safety footwear with metatarsal protection.
Impact-rated eye protection.
Hearing protection where the noise level exceeds 85 dBA.

Additional protection is required for specific tasks. Chemical splash
goggles and a face shield are worn for any work opening a line that has
contained caustic or acid. Breathing apparatus is required for entry to any
space with a measured oxygen content below 19.5 percent.

1.5 Hydrogen sulphide

Personal H2S monitors are worn in all sour service areas. The monitor alarms
at 5 ppm and evacuation is immediate at 10 ppm. A monitor that has alarmed
is not silenced and re-entered; the area is cleared and the reading reported
to the shift supervisor.

2.0 PERMIT TO WORK

2.1 When a permit is required

A permit is required for any work involving breaking containment, hot work,
work at height above two metres, entry to a confined space, excavation, or
isolation of a protective system.

2.2 Validity

A permit is valid for one shift only. Work continuing beyond a shift
requires the permit to be revalidated by the incoming supervisor, who
re-verifies the isolations personally.

2.3 CONFINED SPACE ENTRY

Entry permit EP-series applies to all vessel and confined space entry.
Atmospheric testing for oxygen, lower explosive limit and hydrogen sulphide
is carried out before entry and repeated every hour while the space is
occupied.

Acceptance values for entry are oxygen between 19.5 and 23.5 percent,
hydrocarbon below 5 percent of the lower explosive limit, and hydrogen
sulphide below 5 ppm.

A standby person remains at the entry point for the whole duration of the
entry and maintains continuous communication with those inside. The standby
person does not enter the space under any circumstances, including to
attempt a rescue.

3.0 LOCK-OUT TAG-OUT

3.1 Principle

Energy sources are isolated, locked and tagged before work begins. Energy
sources include electrical supply, stored pressure, stored mechanical energy
in springs and raised loads, and thermal energy.

3.2 Personal locks

Each person working on the equipment applies their own lock. A lock is
removed only by the person who applied it. Where that person is unavailable,
removal requires the written authorisation of the area manager and a
physical verification that nobody remains at the equipment.

3.3 Verification

Isolation is verified by attempting to start the equipment from its normal
control point after the isolation is applied. An isolation that has not been
verified by attempted start is not an isolation.

4.0 EMERGENCY RESPONSE

On hearing the site alarm, personnel proceed to the nearest designated
assembly point upwind of the release. Vehicles are left in place with the
keys in them. Roll call is taken by the area warden and reported to the
incident controller.
