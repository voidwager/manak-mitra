DEMO REFINERY - CRUDE DISTILLATION UNIT II
CENTRIFUGAL PUMP INSPECTION - STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE

Document reference: SOP-CDU2-0412
Revision: 7
Issued: 14 March 2026
Custodian: Rotating Equipment Section

FICTIONAL DOCUMENT. This procedure describes an invented facility and is
provided for software demonstration only. It is not an approved plant
procedure and must never be used to carry out work.

1.0 SCOPE

This procedure covers routine and shutdown inspection of horizontal
centrifugal pumps in service on Crude Distillation Unit II, including the
crude charge pumps P-204 and P-205 and the installed spare P-206. It applies
to both the mechanical inspection carried out during a running survey and
the internal inspection carried out with the pump withdrawn.

It does not cover vertical can pumps, which are addressed by SOP-CDU2-0418,
nor the reciprocating machines covered by the compressor procedure.

1.1 DEFINITIONS

Running survey - measurements taken with the pump in service and the
casing pressurised.

Internal inspection - inspection carried out with the pump isolated,
drained and the top half of the casing removed.

Acceptance limit - the value at or beyond which the pump is withdrawn from
service or an intervention is scheduled, as tabulated in Section 4.

2.0 ISOLATION AND PERMIT

2.1 Electrical isolation

Electrical lock-out tag-out must be applied at the local isolator before any
work begins. The isolator is padlocked in the open position and the key is
retained by the permit holder for the duration of the work. A second person
verifies the isolation independently and countersigns the permit.

Attempting to isolate at the motor control centre alone is not acceptable:
the local isolator is the point of isolation for this unit.

2.2 Process isolation

Suction and discharge isolation valves are closed and the casing is drained
through the low point bleed to the closed drain header. Where the pump
handles material above its auto-ignition temperature, the casing is allowed
to cool to below 60 degrees Celsius before the drain is opened.

Confirm the zero energy state with the area operator and record the permit
number in the inspection log. No inspection may begin against a verbal
confirmation.

2.3 Nitrogen purge

Pumps in hydrocarbon service are purged with nitrogen until the measured
hydrocarbon content at the vent is below 1 percent of the lower explosive
limit. The purge result is recorded on the permit.

3.0 RUNNING SURVEY

3.1 Measurement points

Vibration is measured at the drive-end and non-drive-end bearing housings in
the horizontal, vertical and axial directions. Readings are taken with the
pump at normal operating flow; a reading taken at minimum flow is not
comparable and must be annotated as such.

3.2 Lubrication

Bearing housing oil level is checked at the sight glass with the pump
running. The level sits between the two marks; a level above the upper mark
causes churning and elevated bearing temperature, and is as much a fault as
a low level.

Oil condition is assessed for water ingress by appearance. A cloudy or milky
appearance indicates water and requires an oil change and an investigation
of the housing seals before the pump is returned to normal service.

3.3 Mechanical seal

The seal area is inspected for visible leakage. A plan 11 flush line is
fitted to these pumps; the flush must be confirmed flowing and the flush
line free of blockage before the pump is restarted after any intervention.

A drip rate above five drops per minute requires the flush line to be
inspected and the seal faces assessed at the next opportunity.

4.0 ACCEPTANCE LIMITS

4.1 General

The limits in this section are the acceptance criteria for CDU-II
centrifugal pumps. A reading beyond a limit is not a matter of judgement:
the stated action is mandatory.

4.2 VIBRATION

Overall vibration measured at the bearing housing must not exceed
2.5 mm/s RMS in any direction.

Between 2.5 and 3.2 mm/s RMS the pump may remain in service, but an
alignment check must be scheduled and completed within seven days, and the
pump is added to the weekly monitoring list.

Above 3.2 mm/s RMS the pump is withdrawn from service immediately and the
area supervisor is notified before any restart is attempted.

4.3 BEARING TEMPERATURE

Drive-end bearing temperature operates normally between 40 and 75 degrees
Celsius. A reading above 78 degrees Celsius raises a high-temperature
warning and requires the lubrication check in Section 3.2 to be repeated.
At 85 degrees Celsius the pump trips on high bearing temperature.

4.4 SUCTION PRESSURE

Suction pressure below 1.4 barg indicates a developing net positive suction
head problem. The pump is not to be run below this value; cavitation damage
to the impeller develops within hours and is not recoverable by adjustment.

5.0 INTERNAL INSPECTION

5.1 Impeller

The impeller is inspected for erosion at the vane tips and for cavitation
pitting on the low pressure face of the vanes. Pitting deeper than 1.5 mm
over an area larger than a 20 mm circle requires the impeller to be
replaced rather than dressed.

5.2 Wear rings

Wear ring clearance is measured at four positions and averaged. The design
clearance is 0.35 mm. At an average clearance above 0.70 mm - twice design -
the rings are renewed, as the recirculation loss makes the duty point
unachievable and drives the vibration levels in Section 4.2.

5.3 Shaft

Shaft runout is measured with the rotor supported in its bearings. Runout
above 0.05 mm requires the shaft to be straightened or replaced before
reassembly.

6.0 RECORDS

Every inspection is recorded in the maintenance history with the permit
number, the readings taken, the acceptance decision, and the name of the
technician. An inspection with no recorded readings is treated as not having
been carried out.
