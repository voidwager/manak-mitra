RECIPROCATING COMPRESSOR INSPECTION PROCEDURE

FICTIONAL DOCUMENT. This procedure describes an invented facility and is
provided for software demonstration only. It is not an approved plant
procedure and must never be used to carry out work.

1.0 EQUIPMENT COVERED

This procedure covers the multi-stage reciprocating compressor C-101 and its
associated suction and discharge systems. C-101 is a two-stage, double
acting machine rated at 4.2 MW driven by a synchronous motor.

Stage one draws from the overhead accumulator at 1.1 barg and discharges to
the interstage cooler at 6.8 barg. Stage two discharges to the treating
section at 24.0 barg.

2.0 SUCTION SYSTEM

2.1 SUCTION FILTERS

Suction filter differential pressure is verified at every shutdown and at
each weekly running survey.

The clean filter differential is 0.08 bar. The element is replaced when the
differential exceeds 0.35 bar. Running above 0.5 bar risks collapse of the
element and carry-over of filter media into the first stage cylinder, which
is a cylinder overhaul rather than a filter change.

2.2 Knock-out drum

The suction knock-out drum is drained at the start of every shift. Liquid
carry-over into a reciprocating cylinder is not compressible and will break
a valve or a rod. The level alarm is function-tested monthly and the test is
recorded.

2.3 Intercooler condensate

Interstage cooler condensate is blown down at each shift change and after
any significant change in ambient temperature. Accumulated condensate
reduces the cooling surface and raises the second stage suction
temperature, which shows first as a rising discharge temperature.

3.0 VALVES AND RINGS

3.1 Valve inspection

Suction and discharge valves are withdrawn and inspected at every major
overhaul, and whenever the discharge temperature for a stage rises more than
12 degrees Celsius above its established baseline at constant load.

A leaking discharge valve recirculates hot gas and shows as a rise in that
stage's discharge temperature with no corresponding rise in throughput. A
leaking suction valve shows as reduced capacity with the discharge
temperature roughly unchanged.

3.2 Piston rings

Piston ring wear is assessed by measuring the rod drop. The design rider
band thickness is 9.5 mm. The rider band is replaced when the measured
thickness falls below 6.0 mm, or earlier if the rod drop trend indicates it
will pass that value before the next scheduled opportunity.

3.3 Packing

Rod packing is replaced when the vent rate exceeds 0.4 normal cubic metres
per hour, measured at the packing vent. A rising vent rate with no change in
operating conditions indicates packing wear rather than a process change.

4.0 LUBRICATION AND TRIP SETTINGS

4.1 Force feed lubrication

Cylinder lubrication rate is set at 22 drops per minute per point at full
load. Under-lubrication scores the cylinder liner; over-lubrication carries
oil into the discharge and fouls the interstage cooler.

4.2 Trip settings

The following trips are proved during commissioning and function-tested at
each major overhaul:

Low lube oil pressure - trip at 1.2 barg.
High discharge temperature stage one - trip at 148 degrees Celsius.
High discharge temperature stage two - trip at 155 degrees Celsius.
High vibration - trip at 11.0 mm/s RMS at the frame.
Low suction pressure - trip at 0.4 barg.

A trip that is bypassed for any reason is recorded on the permit with the
reason and the authorising signature, and the bypass is removed before the
machine is handed back to operations.
